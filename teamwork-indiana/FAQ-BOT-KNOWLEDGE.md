## Getting started

### How does someone get started? / What is the Free Performance Assessment?

Every athlete starts with an assessment. It's a real first session, free (a
$99 value). A coach sits down with the athlete/family for goal-setting and
injury-history planning, then we test on the turf with the same technology
used by college and pro programs: Hawkin Dynamics force plates for jump and
lower-body power testing (measuring far more than just jump height), plus
sprint speed testing and first-step acceleration evaluation. The athlete
leaves with personalized training recommendations and zero obligation to
join. Book through the website form, by texting/calling (812) 445-5551, or by
emailing memberships@teamworkbloomington.com.

### What do the force-plate jump tests measure? (parent-friendly)

Our Hawkin Dynamics force plates turn every jump into real data. Three tests:

- **Countermovement Jump — the "game jump."** The athlete dips down quickly
  and jumps as high as they can, like jumping for a rebound or header. The
  plates measure not just how high they jump but how — how much power their
  legs produce and how well they use their natural spring. Best all-around
  picture of explosive athleticism.
- **Squat Jump — the "pure strength jump."** Hold a low squat, pause, jump
  straight up — no dip, no momentum. Removes the spring and shows raw leg
  power on its own. Comparing it to the countermovement jump answers: is this
  athlete more muscle or more spring? That shapes what we train first.
- **Countermovement Rebound Jump — the "quickness jump."** Jump, land, and
  immediately jump again. Measures how fast an athlete gets off the ground —
  the ability behind second-effort rebounds, quick cuts, and explosive first
  steps. Two athletes can jump the same height; the one off the ground faster
  wins the play.

Together the three jumps show how high the athlete jumps, where the power
comes from, and how quickly they can use it — so the plan targets exactly
what will move the needle.

### How young can kids start?

It depends on the kid, not the calendar. We typically start athletes around
age 8, but it's case-by-case. The real green light is whether a kid can stay
engaged and follow coaching for a full session — listening, taking turns, and
moving safely. Some 7-year-olds are ready; some 10-year-olds need a little
more time. Bring them in and we'll tell you honestly.

### Does my athlete need training experience to start?

No — that's the point. Most athletes walk in with little or no formal
training experience, and we want to be where they gain it. Our goal is bigger
than teaching a squat: we want our athletes to walk into any weight room —
middle school, high school, or college — as the leaders: the ones who know
what they're doing, move with confidence, and set the example while everyone
else is learning on the job.

### What should we wear or bring?

Athletic clothes, clean training shoes, and a water bottle. We also recommend
cleats/spikes or turf shoes for change-of-direction work — not necessary for
the assessment, but great to have once training starts. We supply everything
else.

## Training

### What happens in a typical session?

Sessions start with soft-tissue release (foam rolling and other self-massage
tools), then a group dynamic warm-up. From there athletes split off into the
specific qualities their individual program calls for: speed, explosiveness,
strength, conditioning, agility and change of direction, reactivity,
plyometrics, and medicine-ball ballistic work — the whole menu of athletic
qualities, trained so the athlete can express their skills in competition.

### How many sessions per week should an athlete do?

The goal is building a big "bank" of training over time — the more quality
exposure the better, as long as training stays something the athlete wants to
do. If 4–5 days a week feels like a chore, 2–3 denser focused sessions serve
the athlete better. As athletes gain confidence they usually ask to train
more — a great sign. The right number depends on sport schedule, commitment
level, and recovery, which coaches monitor and adjust. Myth-bust: more
training doesn't cause overuse injuries — poorly designed, unmonitored
programs do. Well-designed, well-coached training builds capacity.

### Is it okay to train back-to-back days?

Usually yes. It depends on the training week/block and season, but when the
choice is back-to-back days or only once that week: come both days. Coaches
adjust what each session asks and the athlete banks two quality sessions.
General rule: more training beats less, and the program flexes around the
family's schedule.

### Is strength training safe for kids?

Yes — when coached properly. Youth programming is movement quality first,
load second, under CSCS-certified supervision.

## Membership & billing

### How much does membership cost?

Memberships start at $129/month for 1 session per week. 6- and 12-month
training blocks earn a price break over month-to-month. Framing: you're
investing in a season of development paid monthly, not renting a gym
month-to-month. (For current plan-by-plan rates, contact us — the bot should
not quote 6/12-month block prices until they're added here.)

### How does billing work?

The first payment is a prorated share of the first month, due on the day of
the first session — starting mid-month means paying only for the part of the
month used. The full membership term then starts on the 1st, billed monthly.
Most families choose a 6- or 12-month training block (price break);
month-to-month is available. Charges appear as **T2 Indiana INC** on
statements.

### What is the 30-Day Risk-Free Guarantee?

If an athlete decides the program isn't the perfect fit in the first 30 days,
they can walk away — no questions, no hard feelings. This is a sign-up
risk-remover and is separate from the membership cancellation policy.

### How do I cancel a membership? (IMPORTANT — exact policy)

- All cancellations MUST be sent by email to
  **memberships@teamworkbloomington.com**. Verbal or front-desk mentions do
  not count.
- 6- and 12-month training blocks: email at least **30 days before the
  billing date**.
- Month-to-month: email at least **two weeks before the billing date**.
- Canceling before a training-block commitment is complete is subject to an
  **early-cancellation fee**.

### What happens when a 6- or 12-month commitment is complete?

The membership rolls over to month-to-month automatically and keeps billing
monthly — the athlete just keeps training. This protects training
continuity: most kids finish their first block and keep going. To stop at the
end of a term, email memberships@teamworkbloomington.com 30 days before the
billing date. Recommend families set a phone/calendar reminder about a month
before their term ends to reevaluate.

### Can we pause for vacation?

Don't pause — bank it. We bank missed sessions so the athlete can double up
in the weeks before leaving and after returning. No cold-turkey stop, no lost
sessions, no restart from zero.

### What happens when my sport season starts?

Keep training. In-season athletes should train at least once per week:
total-body work plus plenty of mobility, flexibility, and soft-tissue care so
tissues stay pliable, fresh, and able to withstand the wear and tear of a
competitive season. Keeping the training stimulus means no starting over from
scratch on strength, speed, and conditioning after the season — and it keeps
athletes healthy when it matters most. Every major sports program in the
country lifts, runs, and trains through its competitive season. Over a
career, the athlete who trains twelve months a year leaves the
six-month-a-year athlete far behind.

## Results

### How fast will we see results?

The promise: measurably faster and jumping higher in 30 days or less.
Everything is baselined at the assessment and re-tested on a schedule —
progress is numbers, not vibes.

### How do you track progress?

At the initial assessment we baseline athletic metrics: sprint times,
acceleration, vertical jump, and force-plate numbers for lower-body power. We
deliberately do NOT test strength on day one — it would be irresponsible to
load an athlete with a barbell before seeing them move; experience is
unknown, and most athletes aren't great at the lifts right away (that's our
job). Strength numbers get tracked once training begins and technique is
earned. The loop: train the qualities → test the progress → reassess the
metrics → recover between sessions. We're not in the business of building
bench-press champions — we'll certainly bench press, but the end goal is
strength, speed, power, quickness, and agility that transfers to the
athlete's sport.

### Do you really train college and pro athletes?

Yes — 300+ collegiate athletes including 100+ NCAA D1, and Teamwork athletes
have gone on to compete in NCAA D1, the NFL, the WNBA, and FIBA. The
signing-day flags on the gym ceiling each mark an athlete's commitment.
