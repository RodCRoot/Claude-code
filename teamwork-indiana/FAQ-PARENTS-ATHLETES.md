## Getting Started

### Who is Teamwork Bloomington for?

Athletes from youth through college who want to get faster, stronger, and
more durable for their sport — and adults who train right alongside them. We
work with athletes across a wide range of sports and experience levels, from
first-time lifters to college-bound competitors. We offer memberships,
private training, group training, short-term programs, plus combines and
testing events for teams.

### What is the Free Performance Assessment?

It's a real first session, free (a $99 value). A coach sits down with you for
goal-setting and injury-history planning, then we test on the turf with the
same technology used by college and pro programs: **Hawkin Dynamics force
plates** for jump and lower-body power testing — measuring far more than just
jump height — plus sprint speed testing and first-step acceleration
evaluation. You leave with personalized training recommendations, and zero
obligation to join.

### What do the force-plate jump tests actually measure?

Our Hawkin Dynamics force plates turn every jump into real data. We use three:

- **Countermovement Jump — the "game jump."** Your athlete dips down quickly
  and jumps as high as they can, just like jumping for a rebound or a header.
  The plates measure not just how high they jump but *how* — how much power
  their legs produce and how well they use their natural spring. It's the
  best all-around picture of explosive athleticism.
- **Squat Jump — the "pure strength jump."** Hold a low squat, pause, jump
  straight up — no dip, no momentum. That removes the spring from the
  equation and shows raw leg power on its own. Comparing it with the
  countermovement jump answers a valuable question: is this athlete more
  *muscle* or more *spring*? That answer shapes what we train first.
- **Countermovement Rebound Jump — the "quickness jump."** Jump, land, and
  immediately jump again. This measures how fast an athlete gets off the
  ground — the ability behind second-effort rebounds, quick cuts, and
  explosive first steps. Two athletes can jump the same height; the one off
  the ground faster wins the play.

Together, the three jumps tell us how high your athlete jumps, where the
power comes from, and how quickly they can use it — so their plan targets
exactly what will move the needle.

### How young can kids start?

Parents ask this all the time, and the honest answer is: it depends on the
kid, not the calendar. We typically start athletes around age 8, but it's
case-by-case. The real green light is whether a kid can stay engaged and
follow coaching for a full session — listening, taking turns, and moving
safely. Some 7-year-olds are ready; some 10-year-olds need a little more
time. Bring them in and we'll tell you honestly.

### Does my athlete need training experience to start?

No — and honestly, that's the point. Most athletes walk in with little or no
formal training experience, and we want to be where they gain it. Our goal is
bigger than teaching a squat: we want our athletes to walk into any weight
room — middle school, high school, or college — as the leaders. The ones who
know what they're doing, move with confidence, and set the example while
everyone else is learning on the job. Confidence and competence are trained
here, just like speed.

### What should we wear or bring?

Athletic clothes, clean training shoes, and a water bottle. We also recommend
**cleats/spikes or turf shoes** for change-of-direction work — not necessary
for your assessment, but great to have once training starts. That's it; we
supply everything else.

## Training

### What happens in a typical session?

Every session follows a rhythm. We start with soft-tissue release — foam
rolling and other self-massage tools — to get muscles ready to move. Then the
group goes through a dynamic warm-up together. From there, athletes split off
into the specific qualities their individual program calls for: speed,
explosiveness, strength, conditioning, agility and change of direction,
reactivity, plyometrics, and medicine-ball ballistic work. In short: the
whole menu of athletic qualities — trained so your athlete can express their
skills where it counts, in competition.

### What makes your approach different?

We assess the athlete and meet them where they're at. We prioritize safety
and movement quality over everything else. We track everything — if our
athletes aren't getting faster and jumping higher, who cares if they're
getting "stronger"? Our coaches truly care about the growth and development
of the athlete, and we re-test regularly so progress is measured, not guessed
at.

### How many sessions per week should we do?

Our goal is to help every athlete build a big "bank" of training over time —
the more quality exposure, the better. But that only works if training stays
something they want to do. If four or five days a week starts feeling like a
chore, an athlete gets far more out of two or three denser, focused sessions.
What we usually see: as athletes gain confidence and experience, they're the
ones asking to come more often — and that's a great sign for their
development. The right number depends on your athlete's sport schedule,
commitment level, and how well they recover between sessions — which we
monitor and adjust as we go. And a myth worth busting: more training doesn't
cause overuse injuries. A poorly designed, unmonitored program does. When
training is well designed, well coached, and watched closely, more training
simply builds more capacity.

### Is it okay to train back-to-back days?

Usually, yes. It depends on what we're chasing that week and where your
athlete is in their training block and season — but when the choice is
between back-to-back days or only making it in once that week, come both
days. We'll adjust what each session asks of them, and they still bank two
quality sessions instead of one. Our general rule: more training beats less,
and a good program flexes around your schedule — not the other way around.

### Is strength training safe for kids?

Yes — when it's coached properly. Our youth programming is built around
movement quality first, load second, under CSCS-certified supervision.
Parents tell us in their reviews: the culture of safety is the point.

## Membership & Billing

### How much does membership cost?

Memberships start at $129/month for 1 session per week. 6- and 12-month
training blocks earn a price break over month-to-month; think of it as
investing in a season of development, paid monthly. Ask us for current block
rates.

### How does billing work?

Simple and fair. Your first payment is a prorated share of your first month,
due on the day of your first session — if your athlete starts mid-month, you
only pay for the sessions left in that month. Your full membership term then
starts on the 1st, billed monthly. Most families choose a 6- or 12-month
training block, which earns a price break — you're investing in a season of
development on a monthly payment plan, not renting a gym month-to-month. (A
month-to-month option is available too.) One heads-up for your bank
statement: charges appear as **T2 Indiana INC** — that's us!

### What's the 30-Day Risk-Free Guarantee?

If an athlete decides our program isn't the perfect fit in the first 30 days,
you can walk away — no questions, no hard feelings. It's that simple: we want
to remove the risk of getting started.

### Going on vacation? Can we pause?

Don't stop — bank it. Rather than pausing cold turkey, we'll bank your missed
sessions so you can double up in the weeks before you leave and after you're
back. Your athlete keeps their momentum, you get every session you paid for,
and nobody restarts from zero.

### What happens when my sport season starts?

You keep training — this might be the most important answer on this page.
In-season athletes should train at least once per week: total-body work plus
plenty of mobility, flexibility, and soft-tissue care, so tissues stay
pliable, fresh, and able to withstand the wear and tear of a competitive
season. It's how athletes stay healthy when it matters most. Just as
important: keeping the training stimulus means your athlete isn't starting
over from scratch on strength, speed, and conditioning when the season ends.
Every major sports program in the country lifts, runs, and trains through its
competitive season — high school athletes should too. Over a whole career,
the athlete who trains twelve months a year leaves the six-month-a-year
athlete far behind.

### How do I cancel a membership?

All cancellations must be sent by email to
**memberships@teamworkbloomington.com** — that's the one channel that counts
(a mention at the front desk can get lost; email protects both of us).
Timing: for 6- and 12-month training blocks, email us at least **30 days
before your billing date**. Month-to-month memberships need at least **two
weeks' notice** before the billing date. Canceling before your training-block
commitment is complete is subject to an early-cancellation fee.

### What happens when my 6- or 12-month commitment is complete?

Your membership rolls over to month-to-month automatically and keeps billing
monthly — your athlete just keeps training, nothing changes. We do it this
way to protect training continuity: most kids finish their first block, love
their results, and keep going without missing a beat. If you do want to stop
at the end of your term, email memberships@teamworkbloomington.com 30 days
before your billing date — and a smart move is setting a reminder in your
phone or calendar for a month before your term ends, so future-you remembers
to reevaluate.

## Results

### How fast will we see results?

Our promise: measurably faster and jumping higher in 30 days or less. We
baseline everything at your assessment and re-test on a schedule, so progress
is numbers, not vibes.

### How do you track progress?

We measure what matters, in the right order. At the initial assessment we
baseline athletic metrics: sprint times, acceleration, vertical jump, and
force-plate numbers for lower-body power. What we deliberately don't test on
day one is strength — it would be irresponsible to load up an athlete with a
barbell before we've ever seen them move. We don't know their experience yet,
and frankly, most athletes aren't great at the lifts right away. That's our
job. Strength numbers get tracked once training begins and technique is
earned. From there the loop runs: train the qualities, test the progress,
reassess the metrics, recover between sessions. And to be clear — we're not
in the business of building bench-press champions. We'll certainly bench
press, but the end goal is strength, speed, power, quickness, and agility
that actually transfers to your athlete's sport. That's why we track athletic
qualities, not gym trophies.

### Do you really train college and pro athletes?

Yes — 300+ collegiate athletes including 100+ NCAA D1, and Teamwork athletes
have gone on to compete in NCAA D1, the NFL, the WNBA, and FIBA. The
signing-day flags on our ceiling each mark one of our athletes' commitments.
Since 2014, more than 1,500 athletes have trained here over 13 years — and
counting.
